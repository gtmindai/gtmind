**AI search visibility tools** track how often ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, Copilot and Google AI Overviews mention your brand or cite your pages. They run a set of prompts on a schedule, record each answer, and report mentions, citations, share of voice against competitors, and sentiment. They measure presence in AI answers, not revenue.

**Key takeaways**

- Every tool here works the same way at its core: it samples prompts and counts how often you appear. The differences are engine coverage, prompt volume, and what the tool helps you do next.
- Numbers disagree between tools because AI answers are unstable. Use them for trends and competitor comparison, not absolute truth.
- Entry prices range from free (one-off checks) to about $400 a month for self-serve plans, with enterprise tiers priced on request.
- Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools now report AI visibility directly, for free. Start there.
- A dashboard doesn't move anything. The value comes from turning gaps into pages and tying citations to pipeline.

A note on neutrality: we're not an AI visibility tracker and have no affiliate relationship with any tool listed. Prices and engine coverage were checked on each vendor's own site in September 2026. This category changes monthly, so check before you buy.

## What does an AI visibility tool measure?

AI visibility is the share of relevant AI answers in which your brand appears. Most trackers break it into five metrics:

- **Mentions.** Your brand name appears in the answer text, with or without a link.
- **Citations.** One of your URLs is linked as a source. This is what drives referral traffic.
- **Share of voice.** Your mentions as a share of all brand mentions across the prompt set, compared with named competitors.
- **Position.** Where you appear in a list answer. Useful for a rough signal, unreliable on its own (more on this below).
- **Sentiment.** Whether the answer describes you positively, neutrally or negatively, and which claims it makes.

Better tools add **source analysis**: which third-party pages (Reddit threads, review sites, listicles) the engines cite when they answer prompts in your category. That tells you where to earn mentions, not just how often you appear.

### What about AI Overview tracking?

AI Overview tracking is a narrower version of the same idea, focused on Google. You can now get part of it for free. Search Console's [generative AI performance report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139?hl=en) shows impressions in AI Overviews and AI Mode by page, country, date and device. It shows impressions only and has no query dimension, so it tells you *which pages* appear but not *for which questions*. Paid trackers fill that gap by running your prompts and recording the Overview itself.

Microsoft offers the equivalent for Copilot. The [AI Performance report in Bing Webmaster Tools](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/February-2026/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview) shows total citations, cited pages and the grounding queries Copilot used to find them.

## Why do AI visibility tools disagree with each other?

Put the same brand into two trackers and you will get two different visibility scores. That's not necessarily a bug. It follows from how AI answers work.

**Answers are non-deterministic.** Ask the same question twice and you get a different answer. SparkToro and Gumshoe had volunteers run identical prompts through ChatGPT, Claude and Google's AI thousands of times and found the odds of [getting the same brand list twice were under 1 in 100](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-recommendations-change-with-nearly-every-query-sparktoro/566242/), and the same list in the same order closer to 1 in 1,000. Rand Fishkin's conclusion was that how often a brand appears across many runs is a steadier metric than its "rank."

**Prompt sets differ.** One tool generates prompts from your keywords, another from an industry database, a third lets you write your own. Ahrefs builds its index from [hundreds of millions of monthly prompts derived from its keyword database](https://ahrefs.com/brand-radar); a self-serve tracker might run your 50 custom prompts. Different questions give different answers.

**Collection method differs.** Some tools query model APIs, others scrape the consumer interface. Peec AI, for example, says it uses [UI scraping rather than APIs](https://peec.ai/ai-instructions) to reflect what real users see. API answers and app answers are not always the same.

**Context differs.** Location, language, logged-in state, memory and personalization all change what a real user sees. Trackers usually run from clean sessions in chosen countries.

**Sampling frequency differs.** A prompt run once a day gives a noisier picture than one run many times a day.

The practical rule: pick one tool, keep the prompt set stable, and compare yourself against competitors *inside that tool*. Trends and relative share are meaningful. A single absolute score is not.

## How to evaluate AI search visibility tools

We compared the ten tools on six criteria. Weight them by your situation. No tool wins on all six, which is why this list isn't ranked.

1. **Engine coverage.** Which answer engines are included in the base price, and which cost extra? At minimum, most B2B teams want ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Gemini and Perplexity.
2. **Prompt volume and control.** How many prompts can you track, and can you write your own, split them by persona, market or country?
3. **Citation and source detail.** Does it show which URLs are cited, yours and third parties'?
4. **Price transparency.** Is there a public self-serve plan, or is it demo-only?
5. **Actionability.** Does it only report, or does it suggest or produce content changes? Does it audit your pages for AI crawlability?
6. **Data access.** API, exports and integrations, so you can join visibility data with analytics and CRM.

## Short reviews of each tool

### Profound

Profound is the most widely cited name in the category and is aimed at enterprise teams. Its [pricing page](https://www.tryprofound.com/pricing) lists a free 7-day trial that runs 50 prompts a day across ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Overviews, using recommended prompts you can't customize. The Enterprise plan covers ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Mode, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, DeepSeek, Claude, AI Overviews and Exa Search, with pricing on request. It also sells agents on a credit system.

**Choose it if** you're a large brand that needs broad engine coverage and has budget for an enterprise contract. **Look elsewhere if** you want to self-serve or start small, which is the usual reason people search for Profound alternatives.

### Peec AI

Peec AI is a tracker focused on clear reporting of visibility, position, sentiment and share of voice. According to [Peec's own published plan details](https://peec.ai/ai-instructions), Starter is $95 a month (as of September 2026) for 50 prompts, Pro $245 for 150 prompts and Advanced $495 for 350 prompts, each with 3 models of your choice. More models are available as upgrades, and Enterprise is priced on request. Some regions see euro pricing on the [pricing page](https://peec.ai/pricing). It covers 80+ countries and collects answers by scraping the consumer interface.

**Choose it if** you want straightforward dashboards and competitor benchmarking at a mid-market price. **Watch** the three-model limit on lower tiers if you need broad coverage.

### Scrunch

Scrunch combines monitoring with what it calls an Agent Experience Platform (AXP), which [generates an AI-friendly version of your site](https://scrunch.com/pricing) for AI crawlers and agents. Starter is $300 a month billed monthly, or $250 a month billed annually (as of September 2026), and includes 350 custom prompts, 3 personas and page audits. Growth is $500 monthly. All plans cover ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, Google AI Mode, AI Overviews and Meta AI, and include agent traffic monitoring.

**Choose it if** you want monitoring and a technical fix for AI crawler readability in one product. **Consider** whether you'd rather fix rendering in your own stack, which we cover in our [LLM SEO playbook](/blog/llm-seo).

### Otterly.AI

Otterly has the lowest paid entry point in this list. [Lite is $29 a month](https://otterly.ai/pricing) (as of September 2026) for 15 prompts across ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity and Microsoft Copilot, with unlimited team members and daily tracking. Standard is $189 a month for 100 prompts and adds API access and GEO URL audits. Google AI Mode, Gemini and Claude are paid add-ons.

**Choose it if** you're a small team or a founder who wants real tracking without a large commitment. **Watch** the add-on costs if you need Gemini or Claude.

### AthenaHQ

AthenaHQ pairs tracking with optimization agents and content recommendations. It has a [free Essential tier](https://www.athenahq.ai/pricing) with starter credits covering ChatGPT, Perplexity, AI Overviews, Gemini and Copilot. The Starter plan is $295 a month (as of September 2026) with 3,600 monthly credits and expands coverage to 11 models, including Claude, Grok, DeepSeek, Meta AI and Mistral. Enterprise is priced on request.

**Choose it if** you want suggestions for what to change, not only a report. **Note** that a credit-based model makes costs depend on how much you run.

### Ahrefs Brand Radar

Brand Radar takes a different approach from most trackers. Instead of only running your prompts, it maintains an index of AI answers built from Ahrefs' keyword database: [over 449 million monthly prompts](https://ahrefs.com/brand-radar) across AI Overviews, Gemini, Perplexity, ChatGPT and Copilot, with Claude for enterprise users. Custom prompt tracking starts at $50 a month and is included with paid Ahrefs plans from Lite up. The full AI Visibility Index is a $199-a-month add-on (as of September 2026).

**Choose it if** your team already lives in Ahrefs and wants market-level visibility data next to backlinks and keywords. **Its strength** is breadth of the prompt sample rather than depth on a few prompts.

### Semrush AI Visibility Toolkit

Semrush's AI visibility product is the [AI Visibility Toolkit](https://www.semrush.com/pricing/ai/). It costs $99 a month per domain, billed annually (as of September 2026) and includes AI visibility reports for any domain, 25 custom prompts tracked daily, competitor analysis, prompt research and an AI-readiness site audit. It reports mentions from ChatGPT, Google AI, Gemini and Perplexity. Semrush also offers a [free AI visibility checker](https://www.semrush.com/free-tools/ai-search-visibility-checker/) limited to three uses a day with no account.

**Choose it if** you already use Semrush and want AI visibility in the same place. **Watch** the 25-prompt limit on the base plan.

### SE Visible (SE Ranking)

SE Visible is SE Ranking's standalone AI visibility product. It tracks [ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Gemini, Perplexity and AI Overviews](https://visible.seranking.com/) on all plans, with Claude listed as coming. Basic is $99 a month (as of September 2026) for 200 prompts and 3 projects; Core is $189 for 450 prompts; Plus is $355 for 1,000 prompts. Annual billing is 20% cheaper.

**Choose it if** you want the most prompts per dollar among the self-serve tools here, especially for agency or multi-brand work.

### Goodie

Goodie pairs tracking with a monthly allowance of optimizations. [Core is $399 a month](https://higoodie.com/pricing) (as of September 2026) for 5 AI models and 120 prompts, with a 7-day trial and strategist support. Pro is $999 a month for 8 models and 250 prompts, and adds SKU-level agentic commerce visibility. Enterprise covers up to 13 engines, including Amazon Alexa for Shopping.

**Choose it if** you're a brand, particularly in ecommerce, that wants tracking plus hands-on help. **It's** the priciest self-serve entry point in this comparison.

### A free option: HubSpot AI Search Grader (plus the platforms' own reports)

HubSpot's [AI Search Grader](https://www.hubspot.com/ai-search-grader) is free, needs no account and takes a couple of minutes. It asks ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini how they see your brand and scores sentiment, presence quality, brand recognition, share of voice and market competition. It's a one-time snapshot based largely on what the models know, not ongoing prompt tracking. HubSpot sells ongoing tracking separately.

Pair it with the two free first-party reports: Search Console's [generative AI report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139?hl=en) for AI Overviews and AI Mode impressions, and Bing's [AI Performance report](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/February-2026/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview) for Copilot citations. Together they cost nothing and cover more than most teams look at today.

**Choose this** if you're establishing a baseline before paying for anything.

Rather than a single "best," match the tool to your situation:

## How to track brand mentions in ChatGPT (without a tool)

If you're not ready to pay, you can track ChatGPT visibility by hand:

1. **Write 20–30 prompts** your buyers ask, split into problem, category and brand questions.
2. **Run each prompt 3–5 times** in a clean, logged-out session. Record whether you're mentioned, whether you're cited, and which competitors appear.
3. **Log the cited URLs**, yours and third parties'. Those sources are your outreach list.
4. **Check GA4 for referrals.** ChatGPT search adds [`utm_source=chatgpt.com`](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12627856-publishers-and-developers-faq) to outbound links, so you can see which cited pages actually send visits.
5. **Repeat monthly** with the same prompts.

This is slow, but it teaches you how answers in your category behave before you buy a tool, which makes you a much better buyer.

## After the dashboard: turning visibility gaps into pages

This is the part none of the vendor listicles cover, because it's not what their tools sell. A visibility score going from 18% to 24% is not a business result. Here's how to turn the data into one.

**1. Sort gaps by commercial intent.** A prompt like "best marketing attribution software for B2B" is worth more than "what is attribution." Rank lost prompts by how close they sit to a purchase.

**2. Read the sources the engines cite instead of you.** If ChatGPT cites a competitor's comparison page and two Reddit threads, you know what to build and where to participate.

**3. Build or fix the page that should win.** Usually that means a comparison page, a pricing explainer or a use-case page, written answer-first with a table and a dated update line. Our [LLM SEO playbook](/blog/llm-seo) covers the structure, and the crawler checks that come before it.

**4. Check that AI crawlers can read it.** Several tools here include page audits for this. A page that renders only in JavaScript won't be cited no matter how good it is.

**5. Tie citations to pipeline.** Track AI-referred sessions by landing page in GA4, carry the source onto the lead, and follow it into the CRM. Then report visibility as a leading indicator underneath pipeline, not as the headline number. Our guide to [SEO KPIs](/blog/seo-kpis) lays out that hierarchy.

This is where gtmind sits, and it's worth being plain about what it is and isn't. gtmind is not a prompt tracker and doesn't replace the tools above. It's a GTM brain: it joins Search Console, GA4 and your CRM into one model and prices each finding against your own closed-won deals. Its GEO agent watches ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and AI Overviews and drafts the pages that get cited, with every change reviewed and reversible. It fits the same family as the [AI marketing agents](/blog/ai-marketing-agents) that act on data rather than only report it.

An illustrative example of the kind of play that joined data surfaces: a tracker shows you're absent from answers to a "vs competitor" prompt. On its own, that's one gap among hundreds. Joined with CRM data, it turns out deals where buyers had visited your existing comparison page closed at a higher rate than average. That moves the refresh of that page to the top of the list, with a dollar estimate attached, ahead of higher-volume prompts with no revenue history behind them.

## Final word

AI search visibility tools are useful instruments with honest limits. They sample an unstable system, so read them as trends. Match the tool to your team size, stack and engines, start with the free first-party reports, and judge any tool by whether it helps you ship the next page, not by how impressive the dashboard looks.
